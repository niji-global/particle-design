From one warehouse or section

On stocktake days, and on days you look for a cask.

Where a long-untouched cask sits may be recorded in one person’s memory and a spreadsheet, and nowhere else. Even in a warehouse like that, the ledger can build up alongside the usual work of moving, counting and measuring.

Count as you walk the racks

Walk the aisle with the RFID reader and it picks up the tags on the casks as you go. The count of casks read rises as you walk, and the casks not yet read stay on the list.

How far and how reliably tags read depends on the tag and on how the casks are stored.

Find a cask by getting closer

Pick a cask by number or from the list, and walk with the RFID reader pointed ahead. The closer you get, the higher the reading on screen and the faster the beep.

Enter a measurement once, at the cask

Volume and strength go into the handheld app, right at the cask. They reach the screen in the office with the method and the time.

Whoever takes over can read it back

Fill date, measurements, transfers and samples drawn line up for each cask.

Shared by section

Stocktakes split by section, with a reason for each discrepancy.

As warehouses and sections are added, stocktakes can still be split by section on the same ledger.

Set up a stocktake for each section

In the office portal, choose the section to count and the device to read with, and set up a stocktake. With a stocktake for each section, the work can be shared between people.

A reason for each cask not found

A cask that was not found, or was in a different place, is given a reason on the device in the warehouse or in the office portal. The person responsible signs it off in the office portal. Corrections and cancellations stay in the history, each with its reason.

See the warehouse as a plan and in 3D

See the layout of the rows as a plan, and the racks down to each tier in 3D. The colour of a cask shows its status (filled, empty, or to be checked).

To write from the warehouse, a device must be approved

New devices and RFID readers can be used once an administrator has checked them. You sign in to the office portal with two-step verification.

What it comes down to

The systems you use now stay as they are.

  • It does not replace your current ledger or production system. It starts with stocktakes on site and checking locations.
  • Start with one warehouse or one section, and widen to work shared by section.
  • Whether tags can be read is checked before you buy, in an on-site read check in your own warehouse.
  • Records are corrected without being erased, and the reason is kept too.

Frequently asked questions

Can every cask be read by RFID?

It depends on how the casks are stored. We check this in the on-site read check, before you buy. Racks that read poorly are managed with the QR code on the tag or the cask number, together with the location record.

Can we use it alongside our current ledger or production system?

It does not replace them. It starts with stocktakes on site and checking locations. How to bring in the lists you use now, and how to connect, is decided case by case as we go through the items.

Can it serve as our statutory records?

No. It is a tool that helps you record and reconcile. Please keep your statutory records as they are.

Does it show how a cask is maturing?

No. RFID is for telling casks apart and cannot measure maturation. The volume and strength you measure are kept as records.
