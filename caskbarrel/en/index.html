as you move, count, measure and taste.

You record at the moments you already move, count, measure or taste. In the warehouse you use the handheld app on an Android device with an RFID reader. In the office you use the web portal.

When the work stops, in front of a cask

  • Where did we put that cask?

    Where a long-untouched cask sits is recorded in one person’s memory and a spreadsheet, and nowhere else. On that person’s day off, someone checks the racks from one end to the other.

  • The ledger and the racks do not match.

    Numbers are read by eye, written on paper and checked against the ledger back in the office. Which casks are missing becomes clear after that.

  • Why did we choose this cask last time?

    The tasting notebook and the cask ledger are kept apart, and the reason for a decision stays in someone’s head. When that person changes, it can no longer be traced.

Keeping track

Know where every cask is, from the ledger.

  1. Count as you walk the racks

    Walk the aisle with the RFID reader and it picks up the tags on the casks as you go. The count rises as you walk.

    How far and how reliably tags read depends on the tag and on how the casks are stored.

  2. The casks not yet read stay on the list

    Go straight from that list to look for them. Casks in places where tags are hard to read can be checked from the location record.

  3. The closer you get, the faster the beep

    Pick a cask by number or from the list, and walk with the RFID reader pointed ahead. The closer you get, the higher the reading on screen and the faster the beep. In the diagram, the rings draw closer together.

Correct without erasing, and keep the reason

Corrections and cancellations stay in the history. A cask not found in a stocktake is given a reason and signed off by the person responsible.

Before rollout

Whether tags can be read is checked warehouse by warehouse.

How well tags read changes with how casks are racked or stacked. So before you buy anything, we run an on-site read check in your own warehouse or cellar: test tags, the same area read several times, and the results compared.

Racks that read poorly are managed with the QR code on the tag or the cask number, together with the location record. You can also keep RFID to the racks that read.

Protecting the ledger

To write from the warehouse, a device must be approved.

  • Everyone signs in to the office portal with two-step verification.
  • In the warehouse, devices and RFID readers must be approved by an administrator before they can write to the ledger.
  • Each company’s data is stored separately.
  • Records are not erased. Corrections and cancellations are kept, each with its reason.

Frequently asked questions

What does beta mean?

It is the version that early customers use while development continues. Screens and functions may change.

When can we start?

Conversations can start straight away. The on-site read check is scheduled after the conversation. Rollouts follow in turn as our preparations are completed.

Can every cask be read by RFID?

It depends on how the casks are stored. We check this in the on-site read check, before you buy. Racks that read poorly are managed with the QR code on the tag or the cask number, together with the location record.

What hardware do we need?

RFID tags for the casks, an RFID reader, and an Android device for the handheld app. For the bottles in the lab, RFID tags for liquid-filled bottles are used (the bottle features are in development). Quantities are decided together after the conversation and the read check.

Can we use it alongside our current ledger or production system?

It does not replace them. It starts with stocktakes on site and checking locations. How to bring in the lists you use now, and how to connect, is decided case by case as we go through the items.

Can it serve as our statutory records?

No. It is a tool that helps you record and reconcile. Please keep your statutory records as they are.

Does it show how a cask is maturing?

No. RFID is for telling casks apart and cannot measure maturation. The volume and strength you measure are kept as records.

Does AI decide blends or predict maturation?

No. The decision is the blender’s.

Can wineries use it?

It handles barrels and lots, topping up, transfers and barrel status. Winemaking differs from winery to winery, so we talk it through and fit the setup to yours.

How much does it cost?

It depends on the number of casks and warehouses, and on the scope. We quote the service fee, tags and hardware, the read check and rollout support separately. For customers who have bought tags and hardware, the service fee is waived for a set period after rollout.
